Vitamin K Dependent Factors

Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1), preventing activation of these factors. Remember: 1972 (Factors II, VII, IX, X and Protein C, S, Z)

II (Prothrombin) VII IX X Protein C Protein S Protein Z

Factor VII has the SHORTEST half-life (~6h) → PT/INR is the first test to become prolonged on starting warfarin. Protein C is also vitamin K dependent with a short half-life — warfarin initially causes a prothrombotic state (transient Protein C deficiency). This is why bridging with LMWH is required.

Key Coagulation Disorders

  • Haemophilia A: Factor VIII deficiency (X-linked) → ↑ APTT, normal PT
  • Haemophilia B: Factor IX deficiency (X-linked) → ↑ APTT, normal PT
  • Von Willebrand Disease: vWF deficiency (carries FVIII) → ↑ APTT (type 2/3), normal PT, prolonged PFA
  • DIC: Consumption of ALL factors → ↑ PT, ↑ APTT, ↑ TT, ↓ Fibrinogen, ↓ Platelets, ↑ D-dimer
  • Protein C Deficiency: Thrombophilia (unprovoked VTE); warfarin-induced skin necrosis risk
  • Factor V Leiden: Resistance to APC (Protein C cannot inactivate Factor Va) → most common thrombophilia in Caucasians
